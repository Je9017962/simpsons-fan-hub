About the Show

The Premise

The Simpsons follows a working-class family in the town of Springfield: Homer, a lovable but careless safety inspector at the nuclear plant; Marge, the patient heart of the household; Bart, a 10-year-old troublemaker; Lisa, a gifted 8-year-old saxophonist; and baby Maggie. The show uses their everyday lives to satirize family, politics, media, and American culture.

History

Created by cartoonist Matt Groening, the family first appeared in short sketches on The Tracey Ullman Show in 1987. The half-hour series premiered on Fox on December 17, 1989. It has since passed 800 episodes, released The Simpsons Movie in 2007, and is now in its 38th season, with Fox renewing it through season 40.

Why It Matters

It is the longest-running American animated series and the longest-running American scripted primetime show. It has won dozens of Emmy Awards and a Peabody Award, and Time named it the best TV series of the 20th century. Homer's "D'oh!" even made it into the Oxford English Dictionary.
